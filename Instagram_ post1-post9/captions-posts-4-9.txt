POST 4 — ONE BOTTLE, DECODED (carousel, 4 frames)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

Clos Cibonne, Tibouren, Côtes de Provence Cru Classé. It looks like a rosé and it is one, technically — but almost nothing about it behaves like the category.

Tibouren is a Provençal grape hardly anyone else bothers with. The estate is one of the few holding the 1955 Cru Classé classification. And the wine is aged in large old chestnut foudres under a film of yeast, the way sherry is — which is where the dried herbs, the orange peel and the faint savoury edge come from.

Serve it around 12°C with something salty and it stops being an apéritif and becomes dinner.

POST 5 — REGION IN FOCUS: CHABLIS (single)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

This is Chablis, in a hand: a piece of Kimmeridgian limestone with a fossil shell set into it, roughly 150 million years old. The whole district sits on a Late Jurassic seabed, and you do not need a geologist to find the evidence — it is lying between the rows.

One grape, Chardonnay. Four tiers, and unusually for wine they are worth understanding: Petit Chablis, Chablis, Premier Cru, Grand Cru, rising with the slope and the sun. Same variety throughout. What changes is where it stands.

Which is why Chablis tastes like Chablis and not like Chardonnay from anywhere else. Austere, precise, faintly saline — and best understood outdoors in February, when the place looks like this.

POST 6 — BEHIND THE CELLAR DOOR (single)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

February in the vineyard. Nothing is growing, nobody is visiting, and the most consequential work of the year is happening.

Pruning is the one decision a grower cannot take back. Every cut sets how many buds the vine carries, which decides the crop, which decides the concentration — months before flowering, a year before anyone opens a bottle. It is done by hand, in the cold, a thousand or more times a day, and it is largely invisible to the people who will eventually drink the result.

We think that is worth showing.

POST 7 — A WINE MOMENT (single)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

We all have one. The bottle someone gave us, or the one we brought back, standing at the back of the rack waiting for an occasion that never quite qualifies.

Here is the uncomfortable part: it is probably not improving. The overwhelming majority of wine is made to be opened within a few years, and the window for the ones that do improve is narrower than people think. A bottle kept for a decade in a warm flat has not been ageing. It has been waiting.

So — Tuesday. Something on the stove, nobody visiting, no reason at all. That is the occasion.

What is yours waiting for?

POST 8 — USEFUL KNOWLEDGE: ACIDITY (carousel, 3 frames)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

If you learn one thing about wine this year, make it this one.

Acidity is the most useful word in wine and the most badly explained. It isn't sourness. It's the reason a glass feels fresh rather than heavy, the reason your mouth waters, the reason you want another sip — and the reason a bottle works with dinner or fights it.

Rich food generally wants more of it; delicate food generally wants less. Not a rule — plenty of great pairings break it — but as instincts go it will do more for your ordering than any score out of a hundred.

POST 9 — TRAVEL: RIBEIRA SACRA (single)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

Look for the figure in this photograph. He is standing on a terrace above the river Sil, and he gives you the scale of the thing.

Ribeira Sacra, inland Galicia: vineyards terraced into the walls of the Sil and Miño canyons at gradients no machine can hold. Everything is done by hand — pruning, harvest, carrying fruit up the slope — and some parcels are still most easily reached by water. The Romans terraced here. The vines went on largely unnoticed for most of the last century.

The wine is mostly Mencía: red, light-footed, cool and faintly stony, closer in spirit to the Loire than to the rest of Spain.

Go in September. Stay on the river.
